==========================
Cold-Boot Recovery Model
==========================

Every design decision in the Elementarium is evaluated against an explicit adversarial verification model: **Cold-Boot Recovery at** :math:`T + 10^5\text{ yr}` under complete erasure of linguistic, digital, and institutional continuity.

Observer Assumptions
--------------------

The recovery specification assumes an observer—either biological or an autonomous optical/machine system—possessing:

1. Sensitivity to electromagnetic radiation in or near the fused-silica transmission window (:math:`185\text{ nm} \le \lambda \le 2\,500\text{ nm}`).
2. Spatial perception capable of distinguishing incidence, symmetry, and collinearity on a 2D projection plane or sensor array.
3. **Zero prior shared symbols**: no Latin/Greek alphabets, no Hindu-Arabic numerals, no ASCII/Unicode, no IEEE-754 floating-point conventions, and no SI unit definitions.

Verification Protocol on Mark I
-------------------------------

To validate the Mark I Desktop Prototype prior to monolithic silica fabrication, recovery is tested across two independent tracks:

- **Track A (Zero-Instruction Human Trials)**: Test subjects receive only the unpowered Mark I optical assembly, unmounted fused-silica Brewster flats, and a broadband light source in a darkened room with zero verbal or written instructions. Success is measured by time-to-collimation (``T0 -> T1``), discovery of crossed-Brewster polarization (``T1 -> T2``), and accurate decoding of proportional invariants.
- **Track B (Autonomous Sensor Cold-Boot)**: An unprompted vision/symbolic agent inspects raw camera frames of the projected plates without external documentation, attempting to infer the formal equivalence syntax, bit-lattice ordering, and embedded ``G.1``–``G.601`` constructive DAG engine (``T1 -> T3``).
